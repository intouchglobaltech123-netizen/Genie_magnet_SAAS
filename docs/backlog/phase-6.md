# Phase 6 backlog — SaaS launch

**Goal:** other agencies sign up, set themselves up from the onboarding questionnaire, and pay; Genie Magnet becomes tenant number 1 on the cloud.
**Exit gate (P6-16):** a new agency signs up, answers the required questions and has a working workspace within minutes; it invites a user, connects WhatsApp and Instagram, and adds a client; the trial ends, the subscription is charged and a GST invoice is issued; a downgrade hides an add-on suite without losing data; the platform admin sees the agency's usage and health but cannot open its data without consent; Genie Magnet runs as tenant number 1 on the cloud after migration.

**Settings, not guesses.** The SaaS brand name and domain, the plans' prices and limits, our GSTIN and invoice details, and the trial length are platform settings the platform admin enters (with working defaults for development), never written into the code. Each agency still sets up everything of its own itself. Signing up with Google, email sending (verification and notices), the managed cloud, custom domains' certificates, WhatsApp embedded sign-up and the live billing accounts come in the last step, with our own apps and keys; until then they are switched off or pretend, as before.

## Progress

**P6-01 · Platform console** — **Done.** For the people named in `PLATFORM_ADMIN_EMAILS` (the user menu shows "Platform console"; anyone else is refused, an agency's owner included). Agencies: each with its plan and status (or "not on a plan"), its people, clients, Genie Assistant use this month, storage, last activity and failed background jobs — counted inside each agency, so only the numbers leave it, never its records. Each agency's plan can be set, its trial extended, or the agency taken off plans; the agency's own audit log records what the platform did. Plans and settings: the brand name and domain, the trial's plan and length, the grace period for a failed payment, our invoice details (legal name, GSTIN, state, address, SAC, number prefix), and the plans. ADR 0011. `/platform/agencies`, `/platform/agencies/:id/subscription`, `/platform/settings`.

**P6-02 · Plans and entitlements** — **Done.** Five add-on suites — People and payroll, Finance and costing, Management, Operations, Genie Assistant — on top of the core every plan has (sales, clients, delivery, publishing, the portal, invoices, settings). Each plan has its suites, limits (people on the team, clients, Genie Assistant drafts a month, storage) and prices a month in rupees and dollars (unset until the platform admin enters them); the four plans to start with are placeholders. A suite not in the plan is refused by the API ("Management is not in your plan (Starter)…") and hidden from the menu; its data stays, and comes back with a larger plan. Adding a person or a client past the plan's limit is refused, naming the plan; invitations not yet accepted count. Agencies not on a plan — Genie Magnet, the samples and anything made before plans — have every suite and no limits. Settings → Plan shows the plan, what the agency uses against its limits, and the plans to choose from. `/plan`.

**P6-03 · Subscriptions** — **Done.** Signing up starts a trial of the platform's trial plan (14 days to start with). A banner counts down the trial's last week; three days before it ends the people who keep the settings are told, and when it ends unpaid the workspace turns read-only: everyone can still sign in, read everything and export it, and changing anything is refused with what to do, until a plan is chosen. Choosing a plan starts it at once for a month and renews it (pretend billing until P6-04); a payment still failing after the grace period turns it read-only the same way. `/plan/choose`; daily job `billing.daily`.

**P6-04 · Billing** — **Done (our accounts in the last step).** Choosing a plan in Settings → Plan pays for it: in rupees through our own Razorpay account's subscriptions (the plan's price plus GST), or in US dollars through Stripe Checkout for agencies abroad. The agency is sent to the provider's page; when the provider's signed webhook says the payment came in, the plan runs to the end of its period and our invoice is issued — numbered across the platform in each financial year with the prefix from the platform settings, with our details and the agency's (from its own invoice settings), CGST and SGST when the agency is in our state, IGST when it is not, and no GST on a dollar invoice (an export). Each payment issues one invoice however often the webhook comes. Renewals come the same way each month. A failed payment puts the plan past due and tells the people who keep the settings; after the platform's grace period the workspace is read-only until it is paid. A new subscription stops the one before it. The agency sees its invoices under Settings → Plan, each printable; the platform console lists every agency's. Until our accounts are set up (`BILLING_PROVIDER=live` with `RAZORPAY_BILLING_KEY_ID`, `RAZORPAY_BILLING_KEY_SECRET`, `RAZORPAY_BILLING_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`, `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`; webhooks at `/webhooks/billing/razorpay` and `/webhooks/billing/stripe`), payments are pretend: choosing a plan starts it at once and issues the invoice, and each period renews by itself. `/plan/choose`, `/plan/invoices/:id`, `/platform/invoices`.

**P6-05 · Usage metering** — **Done.** What each agency uses is counted inside it — people on the team, clients, Genie Assistant drafts and answers this month, storage (files kept, and those on their way up), and WhatsApp messages sent this month — and shown on Settings → Plan against the plan's limits and in the platform console for every agency. Beyond people and clients, the plan's Genie Assistant drafts a month and its storage are limits: drafting past the month's allowance is refused naming the plan ("…or write this one yourself"), alongside the agency's own AI budget, and an upload that would not fit says how much is used and how much the file needs. WhatsApp goes through each agency's own number, so it is counted, not limited.

**P6-06 · Set-up wizard** — **Done.** A new agency lands on Set up your workspace (`/app/setup`; Home's set-up guide links to it). Step by step: answer the essentials — the agency questionnaire's four required sections (about the agency, services and packages, the main goal, the team); make the packages table the agency's packages; set this year's goals from the revenue target (with its quarters, when given); the suites worth having, each with why, from the answers — management for a main goal, people and payroll for a team of three or more, finance and costing for priced packages, operations for video production or shoot days, Genie Assistant for social media or personal branding — shown against the plan, advice only; and inviting the team. Optional sample data — three clients with a contact each, four leads along the pipeline and four videos, all marked “(sample)”, made through the same services as real ones (so the plan's client limit applies) — with `.test` emails and phone numbers no Indian mobile has, so nothing can reach anyone. Removing it takes whatever the team made for those clients since (videos, agreements, shoots, topics, portal links, draft invoices), by `app_remove_sample`; a sample client an invoice was issued to stays, with its invoices, for the GST records. Home's guide gains WhatsApp Business and the client portal as steps. `/agency/setup/wizard`, `/agency/setup/sample`.

**P6-07 · White-label** — **Done (certificates in the last step).** The agency's logo and brand colour were already on everything its clients see — the portal, questionnaire links, invoices, monthly reports and payslips — and the portal's browser tab now carries the agency's name. In Agency profile, “Use it in your team's app too” puts the brand colour across the team's own app — buttons, links, focus rings, the side menu and charts, in light and dark mode, darkened where needed so text on it stays readable — with the agency's logo and name at the top of the menu. Your portal address: the agency adds its own sub-domain (portal.youragency.com) and two DNS records — a CNAME to the platform and a TXT record proving the address is its own; Check now looks them up, and once the TXT record is seen the portal and questionnaire links it sends use that address (`/c/<token>`, `/q/<token>`). On any host not in `APP_HOSTS` the web app serves only those client pages and the API calls they make. The server issuing certificates asks `GET /domains/allowed?domain=` first, so only verified addresses get one; setting that up comes with production hosting. `PORTAL_CNAME_TARGET` names where the CNAME points (the web origin's host by default). `/agency/portal-domain`, `/agency/portal-domain/check`.

**P6-08 · Support access** — **Done.** The platform's support team cannot see an agency unless the agency lets it in: Settings → Support access, for one hour to three days, seeing only or also fixing, with what to look at — and taken back at any time. The platform console shows which agencies have let support in; someone from the platform's team enters from there under a sealed cookie bound to them, which works only while the consent lasts (taking it back or the time running out ends the visit at once, on the next request). While in, a banner says whose agency it is, how far and until when, with a way out; salaries and payroll, people's personal planners and the client portal stay closed to support at any level, and the team and roles are seen only; support can never change its own access. Each visit's start and end, and every change made, are in the agency's audit log, marked as support. `/support-access`, `/support-access/:id/revoke`, `/platform/agencies/:id/support`, `/platform/support`.

**P6-09 · Feature flags and announcements** — **Done.** In the platform console's Plans and settings: announcements — a title, a message, information or warning, the first and last day, and every agency or only those on some plans — shown above every page to the agencies chosen, each put away by the person in their browser; and feature flags — a key and what it switches, on for every agency or only for the agencies chosen — which the app reads with `useFlag(key)` (the API sends the flags on for the active agency with who is signed in).

**P6-10 · Data portability** — **Done.** Settings → Your data, for the agency's owner. Export everything: one ZIP made in the background in a minute or two (the owner is told when it is ready) — a CSV for each table, with a byte-order mark so Excel reads it as UTF-8, all of it again in `data.json`, and a README. It holds the agency's own records only, read through its own row-level security; passwords, sign-in tokens, keys and secrets, bank account and PAN numbers, each person's own financial planner and the background work queue are left out, and uploaded files are listed, not included. Delete the workspace: the owner types the agency's name; the workspace is deleted 30 days later, and until then a banner on every page says when, the people who keep the settings are told, and the owner can stop it. When it is due, the hourly sweep removes everything the agency owns — every table with its records, by `app_purge_agency` (a database function that refuses an agency not due), and its stored files — and nothing of anyone else; the platform's invoices to the agency are kept for our accounts. `/data/exports`, `/data/exports/:id/download`, `/data/deletion`.

**P6-12 · Marketing site and pricing page** — **Done (the domain and launch in the last step).** `/welcome` (what it does for a content or video agency, the three stages from the first call to the last invoice, and the plans) and `/pricing` (each offered plan with its description, price a month plus GST and in US dollars when set — “Price on request” until the platform admin enters one — its limits and suites, the core every plan has, and what each suite holds). Both are rendered on the server from `GET /public/site`, the public part of the platform settings — brand name, domain, trial and offered plans, never our invoice details, flags or announcements — at most five minutes old, so the brand name, plans and prices change from the platform console without a release; without the API they show the defaults. Sign-up and sign-in are linked from both. They stay out of search engines, like the rest of the site, until launch, when the root moves from the clickable demo to `/welcome`.

**P6-14 · Security and load** — **Done (the penetration test before launch).** A limit for each agency as a whole (`RATE_LIMIT_AGENCY_PER_MINUTE`, 6,000 a minute) on top of each person's limit on each route, so one agency cannot slow the platform for the others. Security headers on every API answer and web page; private-link pages (the client portal, questionnaires) send no referrer, so their token never leaves with a link a client follows; the API's interactive docs are off on servers with real data (`API_DOCS`). The OWASP Top 10 review is in `docs/security/owasp-review.md`, with what is left for later (a Content Security Policy with production hosting; email confirmation, two-factor and Google sign-in in the last step; dependency alerts in CI). `scripts/load-test.mjs` runs ten times Genie Magnet's load — 120 people at once — and passed on the development laptop with a 95th percentile of 23 ms and no errors (30 times: 87 ms); `scripts/verify-restore.mjs` checks a restored database against the live one (tables, rows, row-level security and policies, grants, functions, migrations), and the first drill passed — and caught a copy with security switched off. Both are in `docs/security/load-test-and-restore-drill.md`, to be run again on the production host.

**P6-15 · Support** — **Done.** Help in the top bar opens the guide for the page you are on, all the guides, or a message to support from that page. The help centre (`/app/help`, also in the side menu) has thirteen short guides — setting up, the team and roles, clients and the portal, sales, production, publishing, invoices, WhatsApp, people and payroll, goals and reviews, Genie Assistant, the plan, and your data — searchable, each with a way to write to support. Messages to support (`/app/support`): anyone on the team writes, with what it is about and the page it came from; each person sees their own, and those who keep the settings see all of the agency's. The support team answers from the platform console's Support tab (waiting ones first), its reply written in the agency's own records; the person who wrote is told, and the conversation carries on until either side closes it. It works while a workspace is read-only. `/support/tickets`, `/platform/support/tickets`.

**P6-16 · Exit gate** — **Done, for everything before the last step.** `apps/api/src/exit-gate/phase-6.e2e.test.ts` runs the story on a brand-new agency: Lotus Media signs up (email and password), starts its trial, answers the required questions, makes its packages and this year's goals and is told which suites fit — all within minutes; it invites an editor, who signs up and joins, connects its WhatsApp number and a client's Instagram, and adds the client; its trial runs out and the workspace turns read-only, it pays for Scale and our GST invoice is issued (IGST, as it has no state on record yet); moving down to Growth hides Management while its goals stay, and moving back brings them back; and the platform admin sees its plan, people, clients and health but is refused its data until the agency lets support in, and is out again the moment it takes that back. Left for the last step, with our own apps, accounts and hosting: signing up with Google, live billing accounts, WhatsApp embedded sign-up (P6-11), and Genie Magnet on the managed cloud after its migration (P6-13).

**Password self-service** — **Done (the emails go out with email sending, in the last step).** “Forgot your password?” on the sign-in page asks for a link to choose a new password (the answer never says whether the address has an account; the link works for an hour, once); setting it signs out every other device. Signed in, the profile menu → Change password needs the current one, and can sign out the other devices. SOPs no longer carry a “PSS line”: PSS means password self-service, not part of an SOP.
