# Phase 5 backlog — Add-on suites

**Goal:** an agency runs the whole business on the system: money and costing, people and payroll, and management.
**Exit gate (P5-23):** attendance is imported from the agency's own attendance export, leave is approved, and a month's payroll runs with payslips; expenses are allocated and the true cost per video is shown for a client; a 45-day strategic review is held in the system with the Round Table and its results released; goals and the revenue cascade come from the agency questionnaire, and the business diagnostic and road map are published.

**Self-service, like everything else.** Payroll rules and salary structures, the attendance file's columns, review agendas, and the health and diagnostic formulas are settings each agency fills in itself, starting from Growth OS defaults where there are any. Nothing is asked of Genie Magnet; statutory amounts (PF, ESI, professional tax, TDS) are entered by the agency as its own rules, never assumed by the app.

## Progress

**P5-01 to P5-03 · Cost rates, expenses and true costing** — **Done.** Settings → Costing: each kit's cost for a shoot day and the month's overheads (finance), and each person's monthly cost and working hours from a given day (restricted like salaries — the owner by default; the audit log records that a rate changed, never the amount). Expenses: anyone adds what they spent with a category, vendor (added the first time), GST and what it counts towards (a video, a client, or overheads), then attaches the receipt; they see and change only their own while it waits; finance sees all, approves, or rejects with a reason, and the person is told. The finance role now approves expenses by default. Costing (finance): for a month, each client's month — what it pays (its running agreements' fees) against labour (time logged at each person's rate on that day), shoots (crew time and the kit's day rate once its day has come), approved expenses and a share of overheads by hours — with the margin; and each video's whole cost so far, with time logged while it was in revision shown as rework, against its share of the agreement's fee. People whose time has no rate are flagged. `/expenses`, `/vendors`, `/costing/rates|settings|videos|clients|summary`.

**P5-04 · Collections** — **Done.** Each agreement's invoice is drafted by itself on its billing day — monthly in advance (from its start), monthly in arrears (for the month before), quarterly in advance (three months on one invoice) — once per month, for finance to check and issue; other terms (a 50% advance) are invoiced by hand. Each agency can switch this off in Settings → Invoices (on by default). Finance → Who owes what: each client's unpaid issued invoices by how late they are (not due, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, over 90 days), with a ready WhatsApp reminder to the client's approver carrying the payment link; Genie Assistant's overdue-invoice insights can also draft a nudge. `/collections/ageing`, job `invoices.schedule`.

**P5-05 · The month's money** — **Done.** Finance → Months: for each of the last six months, what was contracted (running agreements' fees), invoiced (before GST), earned (each fee in proportion to the videos delivered against those promised), collected (online and by hand) and spent (time at cost rates, shoots and kit, approved expenses and overheads), with the margin. A month that has ended is closed by finance: its figures are kept as they were, and time and expenses dated in it are refused until it is reopened with a reason (both in the audit log). `/finance/months`, `/finance/months/:month/close|reopen`.

**P5-06 to P5-08 · People, attendance and leave** — **Done.** People: each person's employee record — employee code (as the attendance device knows them), department, designation, kind of employment, joining and leaving dates, contacts and documents (HR only) — kept by HR; each person sees their own. Bank account, IFSC, PAN, UAN and ESI number are kept encrypted for payroll and shown only by their last characters; only those who may change salaries change them. Attendance: imported from the agency's own export (Excel or CSV, from any device) — one row per person and day, or one row per punch, when the first and last punch of each day are kept — matching people by employee code, email or name, with a check report and undo. Each day is read by the agency's own rules (start time, minutes before late, minutes under which it is a half day, weekly offs, holidays); a working day with no record is absent when an import covers it (from its first day to its last), for people on the device (with an employee code, or in an export) and with the agency that day. A person asks for a correction (a missed punch, a shoot elsewhere) and HR approves it; a later import keeps it. Leave: the usual kinds (casual, sick, unpaid) to start, which HR changes — days a year, paid or not, days carried into the next year; weekly offs and holidays in between are not counted; HR sees the shoots and due videos a request clashes with before deciding; approved leave shows on the attendance; balances for everyone. Nobody decides their own leave, correction or expense — someone else does — except the owner, who has no one above them. `/people`, `/people/departments`, `/attendance`, `/attendance/settings|corrections`, `/leave`, `/leave/types|balances`, `/imports/attendance`.

**P5-09 · Payroll** — **Done.** Payroll (for those who may see salaries — the owner by default): each person's monthly salary from a day on, in the parts the agency's payslips show (Basic, allowances…); a later salary applies from its day, and a change in the month pays each for its days. The agency's own rules: what a day's pay is (the days in the month, its working days, or 30), late days that count as half a day without pay, and its deductions and contributions exactly as its auditor gives them — a percentage of chosen parts with an optional ceiling, a fixed amount, slabs by the month's pay, or an amount entered for each person each month (such as income tax), each optionally only up to a monthly salary, and either taken from pay or paid by the agency on top. Nothing statutory is assumed: with no rules, nothing is deducted. Each month's run is worked out from attendance and leave — absent days, half days, unpaid leave, lateness by the rule, and days before joining or after leaving — and payroll checks it, adds a bonus, reimbursement or recovery, the month's amounts and any days without pay the attendance does not show, then locks it. Locking gives each person their payslip (notified, printable, with the net pay in words) and keeps the month's attendance as it was paid: imports, corrections and leave in it are refused until it is unlocked with a reason. The bank's transfer sheet (account, IFSC and net pay, saying who has no account) is for those who approve payroll. The audit log records that a salary changed, never the amount. `/payroll/settings`, `/payroll/salaries`, `/payroll/runs`, `/payroll/runs/:month/refresh|lock|unlock|bank-sheet|payslips/:userId`, `/payslips`.

**P5-10 · Hiring** — **Done.** Hiring (HR): each opening with the role's task document — what the person is responsible for, delivers and does, what to look for (skills, knowledge, self image, traits, motives), the interview's situation-task-action-result questions, where to find people, the hiring manager and the pay range (HR only). Candidates move from applied through screening and interviews; an interview is set up with someone in the team, who is told and sees it on their calendar. Interviewers, the hiring manager and HR fill in scorecards — five ratings of 1 to 5 (70%) and the role's task out of 10 (30%) — and each recommends hire, hold or not taken by the agency's own rule (Hiring rule, with defaults). Once scored, HR asks for approval to hire; someone with HR approval approves (or says no with the reason); the offer (designation, monthly pay, joining day, their role in the workspace) is recorded with the candidate's answer; joining invites them to the workspace with that role, and when they accept their employee record starts from the offer. Hiring managers and interviewers see their candidates without pay; CVs are HR only. `/hiring/settings`, `/hiring/openings`, `/hiring/candidates`, `/hiring/candidates/:id/stage|interviews|scorecard|approval|offer|offer/answer|join`.

**P5-11 · Performance and learning** — **Done.** Each employee record now says who the person reports to and which KRAs their month is scored on. Performance: HR keeps KRA templates — each KRA with how it is measured, its target and weight (adding up to 100), lower-is-better where it applies, and an optional quality gate that caps the score when one KRA falls below a level. A KRA can be filled in by the app: videos the client approved with the person as editor, approved on or before the due day, the quality check passed first time, client revision rounds, shoots done, hours logged, working days present. A person's manager (or the head of their department, or HR — never themselves, the owner excepted) starts the month, enters the rest, may correct a figure, and shares it; the person is told and may reply. The leaderboard ranks the month's shared scores with the change from the month before (managers and HR by default; everyone if the agency says so). The A–C rating by competence (skill, knowledge) and commitment (self image, motive, trait), with the agency's bar; people see their own only if the agency says so. Learning: HR keeps learning paths (modules with hours and links); HR or a manager gives a path to people, who are told and mark modules done. The skill matrix: HR lists the skills, managers set their people's levels from 0 (not yet) to 4 (can teach). `/performance/settings|templates|team|scorecards|leaderboard|ratings`, `/learning/paths|assignments|skills`.

**P5-12 · Daily data sheet** — **Done.** Each agency starts with the usual sheets (Video Editing, Social Media, Technical Support, HR), which HR changes: what the role counts each day besides its tasks (posts done, DMs replied, errors… — mistakes shown in red), an example task, and who signs it in which order (manager then HR, HR then manager, or one of them). Each person's sheet is chosen on their employee record. The person fills in the day — each task (one of their videos, or in words) with its times, done or pending (with why), productive or not — and the day's counts; it adds up the time against the agency's working day and needs the reason when it is more than the agency's slack off. Submitted after the agency's cut-off (or for an earlier day), it is late. The signers sign in order, each told in turn, or send it back with a note; nobody signs their own (the owner excepted); with no manager set, HR signs for the manager. Managers and HR see the team's day: signed, submitted, late, being filled in, missed, on leave, or a day off. `/daily-sheets/settings|templates|team`, `/daily-sheets/day/:date`, `/daily-sheets/:id/sign|send-back`.

**P5-13 · Goals and the revenue cascade** — **Done.** Goals: the company's, each department's serving one of them, each person's serving a department's (or the company's) — with its kind, owners, what is measured, from and to, the start and due days, the STOP rhythm it is reviewed in, and its S.M.A.R.T. wording. A goal can follow a figure the app knows (invoiced, money collected, running agreements' monthly fees, clients won, proposals sent, new leads, videos approved, people in the team) or be entered by hand. Each shows its progress against where it should be by now, and is on track, at risk or off track by the agency's rule (too early to judge in its first days). Owners check in with an update, a breakthrough or a breakdown (with a new figure for a goal entered by hand); a breakdown tells the owners of the goal it serves. Everyone sees their own goals and what they serve; those who may see goals see them all. The revenue cascade takes the agency's own last year — the running book, renewals, new clients' fees, proposals accepted, leads that got a proposal, editing time and the videos promised — and works the year's target back to new sales, clients to win, proposals, leads, advertising, videos a month, capacity used and editors to hire; it is saved, and sets chosen goals' targets. Permissions: the area "Goals, reviews and dashboards" (was "Reviews and dashboards") can now be edited and approved; managers approve and team leaders edit by default. The menu is grouped into Workspace, Money, People and Management. `/goals`, `/goals/settings`, `/goals/cascade`, `/goals/cascade/apply`, `/goals/:id/check-ins`.
